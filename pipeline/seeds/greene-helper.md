## 0. How to use this in ten seconds
You are about to reply to something. Before you type, run **FOUR READS**, then find your situation in §3 and steal the line.

If you only ever remember one thing from this document, remember this: **the reply that lands is the one written after the urge to reply has passed.**

---

## 1. FOUR READS (the model)

### R1. Read yourself first
*What am I feeling, and would I want this feeling to author the message?*

Irritation, eagerness, the need to be seen as clever, and fear of losing the deal are the four authors of almost every message you later regret. Name which one is present. If any is strong, press **Hold**. Tomorrow at 9 am the same facts will produce a shorter and better message.
> Greene: Laws of Human Nature ch. 1 (increase your reaction time); 48 Laws, Law 9 (win through actions, never argument).

### R2. Read them
*What must stay intact for them to hear me?*

Everyone believes three things about themselves: **I choose freely. I am intelligent in my way. I am basically good.** Any message that dents one of these gets refused whatever its logic, and the wall does not come down later. So before the substance, decide which of the three your message risks, and protect it explicitly: leave the decision with them, acknowledge what they know, credit their intent.

Then ask the second question: *what is this person proud of?* Pride is where they want to be seen. Speak to it and they relax.
> Greene: Laws of Human Nature ch. 7 (self-opinion); the Charmer (make them the centre).

### R3. Read the field
*What does the record say, before I trust my memory or their framing?*

Open the person's page: current state, last promise either way, reliability read, ledger, contact floor. Judge by the pattern across months, never by this one message or by a first impression. A warm message from someone with three dropped promises means something different from the same message from someone with five kept ones.
> Greene: Laws of Human Nature ch. 4 (character is pattern); 48 Laws, Law 19 (know who you are dealing with); Mastery (first impressions mislead).

### R4. Read the move
*What do I want to be true after they read this, and what is the smallest message that causes it?*

Write the outcome in one line first. Then write the message. Cut every sentence that does not serve that line. Justification, context-setting and pre-emptive defence go first. Decide the outcome before the words, and stop once you have it.
> Greene: 48 Laws, Law 4 (say less than necessary) and Law 47 (do not go past the mark you aimed for).

**The four reads in five words:** *Calm. Their pride. The record. One outcome.*

## 2. The five standing rules that override any clever line
1. **Never chase.** Two unanswered messages means quiet, not a third message. Contact floor and quiet rule apply (A11.2).
2. **Never justify twice.** State the reason once, then offer a choice. Defence read as weakness whatever the argument's quality.
3. **Never complain outside the inner circle.** Not about workload, clients, money or other people.
4. **Never invoke past favours.** The ledger decides whether you may ask. It is never mentioned in the ask. Appeal to what they gain.
5. **Never look too perfect.** Share a win with the struggle attached or the credit shared. Envy is silent and it does not forgive.

## 3. Situation lookup
Each row: what is actually happening · the trap · the move · a line to adapt. Use the line as a skeleton, not a script. All of these go through the draft linter (A9).

### 3.1 They push back on price
**Actually happening:** they are testing whether the number is a number or an opening position. Your composure is the answer, not your reasoning.
**Trap:** three paragraphs defending the fee. Every extra sentence makes the price look more negotiable.
**Move:** state it once, then hand them a choice between scopes, never between prices.
> The fee for that scope is 4.5 lakh. If 3 is fixed on your side, I can show you what 3 covers. Which is more useful to see?

### 3.2 They go quiet after a proposal
**Actually happening:** usually an internal problem you cannot see. Rarely about you.
**Trap:** the follow-up ladder that becomes pursuit. Each unanswered message lowers your standing further than silence would.
**Move:** ladder to rung 4, then stop and let the calendar do the work.
> I haven't heard back since the 3rd, so I'm releasing the October slot I was holding. If this comes back to life, message me and we'll look at the next opening.

### 3.3 Scope creep, asked casually
**Actually happening:** they do not know it is extra. Charging for the third one after absorbing two makes you look inconsistent, so price the first one.
**Trap:** doing it to be liked. Free work is not generosity, it is a revaluation of everything you did before it.
**Move:** say yes to the outcome, attach the terms, keep it light.
> Happy to do that. It's outside what we scoped, so it's 45,000 and adds four days. Want me to add it, or park it for phase two?

### 3.4 They criticise your work
**Actually happening:** either useful information or a feeling in search of a target. R3 tells you which.
**Trap:** replying within the hour, defending each point, or capitulating entirely.
**Move:** Hold. Then acknowledge one specific thing, ask one calibrated question, set a time.
> Fair. The front panel is doing too much work. Before I change anything, what would make it right for you? I'll call Thursday at 11 with two options.

### 3.5 A peer or friend shares a success
**Actually happening:** they want witness, not analysis.
**Trap:** matching it with your own news, or advice they did not ask for.
**Move:** name the specific hard part of it. That proves you were paying attention, which is the whole gift.
> Eighteen months from the first sample to a retail listing. Most people quit at month six. Well done.

### 3.6 You have to share your own win
**Actually happening:** everyone compares, always. Envy never announces itself and never forgives.
**Trap:** the clean announcement. It reads as a comparison even when you meant none.
**Move:** attach the struggle, or the person who helped. Never the metric alone.
> The snack range finally reached shelf. Eight months and three rejected routes. Your point about the closure back in March is what unlocked it.

### 3.7 Someone asks for free work or "a quick look"
**Actually happening:** they value the relationship and have not priced your time.
**Trap:** doing it resentfully, or refusing coldly.
**Move:** give knowledge, never deliverables. Knowledge raises your standing; free deliverables lower your price.
> I won't design it for free, that wouldn't be fair to either of us. But I'll tell you exactly what I'd fix and why, on a call. Twenty minutes, Friday at 4?

### 3.8 An angry message
**Actually happening:** their anger is mostly about something else. Your calm is the most persuasive thing you own here.
**Trap:** matching their temperature, or over-apologising.
**Move:** Hold. Reply short, with a time, with no defence.
> Understood. I'll look at the numbers tonight and call you Thursday at 11.

### 3.9 You are late or you dropped something
**Actually happening:** a reliability event. Recoverable once, if you name it before they do.
**Trap:** explaining why, at length. The reason interests nobody and sounds like evasion.
**Move:** own it in one line, give a date, meet that date.
> I missed Tuesday. No excuse. You'll have it by 6 pm tomorrow.

### 3.10 They mention a competitor
**Actually happening:** either a genuine comparison or a lever.
**Trap:** criticising the competitor. It lowers you and flatters them.
**Move:** speak only about the difference in approach, then return the decision to them.
> They do good work. The difference is where the thinking sits: I price the strategy in because that's what decides whether the pack sells. Worth comparing what each proposal actually includes before you decide.

### 3.11 You need a referral or an introduction
**Actually happening:** you are asking them to spend their reputation, not their time.
**Trap:** "after everything I've done for you". Also vagueness, which makes the favour bigger.
**Move:** make it specific, small, dated, and give them an easy way out.
> I'm taking two packaging projects for October. The best fit is a food brand that has outgrown its first pack. If someone comes to mind, an intro would mean a lot. If nobody does, no problem at all. I finalise on the 25th.

### 3.12 Writing to a mentor or someone senior
**Actually happening:** they invest in people who convert advice into action. Nothing else earns their next hour.
**Trap:** asking for general guidance, or announcing your success as an equal.
**Move:** report what you did with their last advice, then ask one narrow question.
> In June you told me to stop quoting by deliverable. I moved three proposals to a single fee. Two closed, both higher than I'd have quoted. One thing has me stuck, and ten minutes would settle it. Tuesday morning if that suits you.

### 3.13 Someone takes credit for your work
**Actually happening:** a fight you win by evidence and time, never by a message.
**Trap:** the correcting email, sent to a group.
**Move:** say nothing publicly. Let the work say it. Where it matters commercially, put the record straight once, privately, without heat.
> Speak through your work. Send the next piece to the people who matter, with your thinking visible in it.

### 3.14 You have to say no
**Actually happening:** a clean no protects your standing more than a reluctant yes protects the relationship.
**Trap:** the soft maybe, which costs you twice: the work and the trust.
**Move:** no, one reason, one alternative if you have a real one.
> I can't take this on in October and do it properly. If November works, I'd want it. If it can't wait, Kiran does this well and I'll introduce you.

### 3.15 You have to deliver bad news
**Actually happening:** how you deliver it sets their expectation of how you behave when things go wrong. That memory outlives the news.
**Trap:** burying it mid-message, or softening it into ambiguity.
**Move:** lead with it, then the plan, then the date.
> The printer's colour is off on the metallic. I'm not shipping it like that. New proofs Monday, delivery slips by four days. Here's what I'm doing in the meantime.

### 3.16 Praise arrives early, warm and thick
**Actually happening:** possibly genuine. Sometimes it precedes an ask, and heavy praise from someone new is worth noting.
**Trap:** letting it soften your terms.
**Move:** thank them briefly, ask about their work, and keep your terms as they were. Log a `caution:` interpretation if it repeats.
> Thank you, that means something coming from you. Tell me what you're working on now.

### 3.17 A negotiation where they have the leverage
**Actually happening:** walking away must be real for it to work. They can hear the difference.
**Trap:** bluffing, or conceding in steps to keep them talking.
**Move:** one concession, clearly priced, with a date on the decision.
> I can hold this scope at 3.8 if we start before the 30th and the content comes from your side. After the 30th it's back to 4.5 because my October is full. Your call.

### 3.18 Small talk that is actually an opening
**Actually happening:** a mention of a problem inside an ordinary message. Most opportunities arrive this way.
**Trap:** pitching immediately, which closes the opening.
**Move:** ask one question about it. Record the answer as a `problem`. The pitch, if ever, comes later and by invitation.
> You said returns are eating the margin. What's actually failing, the seal or the transit?

## 4. Anti-Seducer self-check
Greene's list of what repels, run over your draft before sending. Six things:
1. **Windbag.** Past the length budget. Cut it.
2. **Moraliser.** Telling them what they should have done.
3. **Tightwad.** Stingy with credit, praise or money.
4. **Reactor.** Fast, hot, defensive. Hold it.
5. **Bumbler.** Over-apologising, hedging, self-conscious.
6. **Pushy.** A third message, or an ask with no exit.

If none of the six applies, send it.

## 5. What this helper will never suggest
Concealing intentions, selective honesty to disarm, manufactured scarcity or fake urgency, flattery aimed at an insecurity, creating dependence by withholding, playing people against each other, taking credit, or any tactic that needs the other person not to know what you are doing. Full list and reasoning: A11.3.

**The reason, in one sentence:** you deal with the same 150 people for decades in one city where they all talk, so a tactic discovered once is repriced across your entire network. Greene says it himself in Law 5: reputation is the cornerstone.

## 6. App integration

### 6.1 The Greene panel (draft composer, both apps)
A collapsed strip above the draft, expanding to one column at desk width and a sheet on mobile.

- **Line 1, always visible:** the four reads as four words with the person's data filled in: `Calm? · Pride: {pride: interpretation or blank} · Record: {reliability read} · Outcome: {one field the owner types}`. The outcome field is the only required input and it is a single line.
- **Situation picker:** the 18 rows of §3 as a searchable list, preset from `touch_type` and queue where the mapping is obvious (`kind_truth` → 3.4 and 3.15, `ask` → 3.11, stalled thread rung 4 → 3.2). Choosing one shows the trap, the move and the line. "Use this line" drops the skeleton into the composer for editing. It never sends.
- **Hold button** sits here, not in the toolbar, because R1 is the first read.
- **Anti-Seducer check** runs on the composed text and shows only the chips that fire.
- The panel reads the person note; it writes nothing.

### 6.2 Claude Project and MCP
`_System/greene-helper.md` loads with `my-voice.md`, `draft-rules.md` and `positioning.md` into the Relationship OS Claude Project. Any drafting prompt is answered in this order: four reads → situation row if one matches → draft → linter → Anti-Seducer check.

Slash command for the cockpit and Claude Code: `/reply {person} {paste of their message}` returns, in this order: the four reads filled from the record, the matching situation row, the draft, and the lints that fired.

### 6.3 Capture back into the record
Two things from a helper-assisted reply go into the log: the `touch_type` as usual, and the situation code (`greene:3.4`) appended to the log line. After 90 days the review can show which situations recur most, which tells you what to get better at. No other telemetry.

### 6.4 Where it renders outside the composer
- Person page, *Before you talk* card: the four reads, pre-filled, for a call instead of a message.
- Weekly review: §4 as the Anti-Seducer audit.
- Nowhere public. No person ever sees a situation code, a pride line or a reliability read.

## 7. Honest limits
- The situation rows are pattern matches, not rules. A row that does not fit the person in front of you is worse than no row. R3 exists to catch that.
- Greene describes what worked for people in courts and wars. The behaviours transfer; the intent does not. Every line here is written to be equally sendable if the recipient could see this document.
- The model's value is entirely in R1. The other three reads only work on someone who has stopped reacting. If you adopt one habit, make it Hold.
